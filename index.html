Every story has a fracture.

Four detective cases built around testimony and physical clues. Question the alibis. Compare the evidence. Find the statement that cannot be true.

“I never went into that room. Not once, all night.”
He says it evenly. In your hand is a cufflink taken from the dead man's fist.

How it plays

  1. Interrogate. Pick a person, ask a question. You get an answer, and most of it will be true.
  2. Present evidence. Set a piece of physical evidence against a specific statement. Get it right and you watch them change their story. Get it wrong and they deny you politely.
  3. Accuse. Name the killer, then hand over the one item that ties them to the scene. Both have to be right.
